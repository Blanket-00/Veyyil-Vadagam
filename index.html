Our Story

A terrace, a cotton saree, and one family recipe.

Vadagam has never come from a factory in our house — it comes from the terrace, where cotton cloths are spread out every morning and taken in every evening, rain or shine, for the better part of a week.

The recipe is my grandmother's: urad dal ground fresh, folded with onion, garlic, and spice, then shaped by hand into small vadagams no bigger than a coin. What we sell is exactly what she used to make — nothing engineered for shelf life, nothing rushed for scale.

— Still made by hand, one courtyard at a time.
How It's Made

Six days, mostly weather-dependent.

Every batch follows the same rhythm — there's no way to speed up the sun, so we don't try to.

01

Soak & Grind

Urad dal is soaked overnight and ground fresh into a coarse batter with onion, garlic, and whole spices.

02

Shape by Hand

The batter is pressed into small vadagams by hand, one at a time, and laid out on cotton cloth.

03

Sun-Dry & Turn

Spread on the terrace from morning to evening for 3–4 days, turned by hand so both sides dry evenly.

04

Sort & Pack

Once fully dried and brittle, each vadagam is sorted by hand and packed airtight, ready to fry and use.
